Read this before you change anything that talks to Microsoft, or when a Microsoft feature stops working. The platform uses two Microsoft tenants: BISO's own, for staff, and BI's, for students. Each section below says what one part does, where its code is, which variables it reads, what people see when it fails, and how to test it. [Secrets and environment](/developers/keep-it-running/secrets-and-environment#microsoft) lists every variable, and [Rotate a secret](/developers/keep-it-running/secrets-and-environment#rotate-a-secret) says how to change one.

- Where each app registration lives, and who owns it: <Private name="azure-app-registration" label="Azure app registrations" />
- Who administers BISO's tenant: <Private name="microsoft-tenant-admins" label="Microsoft tenant administrators" />

## Staff sign-in

**What it does.** Staff sign in to the admin with their BISO Microsoft account, through Appwrite's Microsoft provider. At each sign-in, the admin copies the person's `SG-App-*` groups into Appwrite teams, and saves the list of them in `m365_team_sync`, using the person's own Microsoft token. Docs reuse the admin's session. [Auth and roles](/developers/change-it/auth-and-roles#how-staff-sign-in) has the whole flow.

**Code.** `signInWithAzure()` in `apps/admin/src/lib/server.ts`, the return route `apps/admin/src/app/(auth)/auth/oauth/route.ts`, and the group sync in `apps/admin/src/lib/m365-sync.ts`.

**Credentials.** The client id and secret are set on the Microsoft provider in the Appwrite console. They aren't variables. The secret probably expires on 5 March 2027: check the exact date in the Azure portal.

**When it fails.** Staff land back on the sign-in page under **Innlogging mislyktes. Prøv igjen eller kontakt support.** (Sign-in failed. Please try again or contact support.) If only the group sync fails, they're signed in anyway, with their teams and saved team list as the sync left them. A failure halfway can leave the two out of step until the next sign-in ([Auth and roles](/developers/change-it/auth-and-roles#how-staff-sign-in)). [Sign-in to the admin fails](/developers/keep-it-running/when-things-break#sign-in-to-the-admin-fails) is the runbook.

**Test it safely.** Your own Appwrite project, as in [Local setup](/developers/change-it/local-setup#point-the-apps-at-your-own-appwrite-project), has no Microsoft provider until you add one. The screenshot scripts show a way round it: they make staff users and sign them in with the project's API key ([Signing in](/developers/change-it/local-setup#signing-in)). The sync has unit tests in `apps/admin/src/lib/m365-sync.test.ts`.

## Microsoft Graph

**What it does.** One app registration with application permissions calls Microsoft Graph without a signed-in person. It's used for:

- the admin's **IT** pages: finding, creating and changing Microsoft accounts and their groups, resetting MFA, ending sign-in sessions, the board turnover, and the department audit at `/it/users/audit`
- interview scheduling: the panel's free and busy times, the Teams meeting and the Outlook invite, from the admin's interview tools and from the website's booking page
- finding who approves an expense, in the api
- the API's admin routes for Microsoft accounts, which nothing in the website or the admin calls ([The API](/developers/change-it/auth-and-roles#the-api))
- `backfill:m365-team-sync`, which reads every staff user's groups to write their saved team list. For that the registration needs the `GroupMember.Read.All` or `Directory.Read.All` application permission ([The saved team list](/developers/change-it/auth-and-roles#the-saved-team-list)).

On the IT pages, global admins may do everything. Campus admins may look up accounts and create new ones, only in the campuses they manage. Everything else, the board turnover included, is for global admins ([Microsoft accounts on the IT page](/developers/change-it/auth-and-roles#microsoft-accounts-on-the-it-page)).

**Campus mail lists.** The admin, and the API's user routes, set a new account's Office field (`officeLocation`) to its campus name, and the IT page's profile form changes it when someone moves campus (`apps/admin/src/app/(portal)/_actions/it-users.ts`). On 29 October 2026 the owner decided to turn each campus's mail list, and the national one, into a dynamic distribution group in Exchange Online that filters on that field. The change is made in Exchange, not in the repo, so check in the Exchange admin centre whether a list is dynamic yet. Once it is, a new account joins it within about 24 hours, and a change of Office moves the person to the other list. Until then, the list keeps the members it has, and someone adds new accounts by hand. A dynamic list's Office value must match the campus name in Appwrite's `campus` table, so check the lists if a campus is ever renamed.

**Code.** `packages/connectors/src/azure/`: `graph-client.ts`, `users.ts`, `authentication-methods.ts`, `calendar.ts` and `recruitment-scheduling.ts`. The admin builds its client in `apps/admin/src/lib/it/graph.ts`. Interview scheduling is called from `apps/admin/src/app/(portal)/_actions/interviews.ts` and `apps/web/src/app/actions/booking.ts`.

**Variables.** `AZURE_GRAPH_TENANT_ID`, `AZURE_GRAPH_CLIENT_ID` and `AZURE_GRAPH_CLIENT_SECRET`, with fallbacks to older names:

- The IT pages fall back to `AZURE_TENANT_ID` and `AZURE_APP_ID` for the tenant and client id, but never for the secret.
- Interview scheduling also falls back to `AZURE_CLIENT_ID` and `AZURE_CLIENT_SECRET` (`readGraphCredentials` in `recruitment-scheduling.ts`).
- The website's booking page needs all three values, though `apps/web/.env.example` lists only the secret.

**When it fails.**

- The IT user list shows the error in its place, for example `Missing Microsoft Graph server credentials`. The api's approver lookup fails with the same message.
- A booked interview is still saved, but no Teams meeting or Outlook invite goes out. If the Graph call fails, the website logs `[Booking] Calendar invite failed:`. With the variables missing, nothing is logged, and the booking page also offers times without checking the panel's calendars. Either way, after a booking without an invite the website emails the staff member who made the booking link, writes a `recruitment.booking.invite_failed` row to `audit_logs`, and the interview shows **Invitasjon ikke sendt** (Invite not sent) on the **Interviews** tab of the vacancy's applications. That email needs `SMTP_HOST` and `SMTP_FROM` on the website. If it can't be sent, the website logs `[Booking] Could not alert staff about a failed invite:`.
- Staff sign-in doesn't use this registration, so an expired Graph secret never stops anyone signing in.

**Test it safely.** No test tenant is recorded, so every Graph call with this registration acts on BISO's real accounts. Pages that only read, such as the user search, change nothing. Never test anything that writes. Creating a user, changing groups, resetting MFA or running a turnover changes a real person's account. Booking an interview, on a local website or with the admin's interview tools, creates a real Teams meeting and sends real Outlook invites to the panel and the candidate. Even the read-only pages use production credentials: get them from the account owner for the session, and don't keep them in `.env.local` afterwards. The unit tests are in `packages/connectors/src/azure/*.test.ts` and `apps/admin/src/lib/it/`.

## Unit boards

**What it does.** The board on each unit page, and the campus leadership on the website, come from Microsoft 365. The api's `GET /api/campus/{campusId}/{departmentId}/board` asks Graph for enabled users whose office location is the campus and whose department starts with the unit's name, with their photos. The website caches each board with Next.js's `hours` cache profile (`apps/web/src/lib/data/unit-board.ts`). So a board follows each person's department and office in Microsoft, and a change there reaches the website once the cache expires.

**Code.** `apps/api/src/app/api/campus/[campusId]/[departmentId]/board/route.ts`, and `apps/web/src/lib/data/unit-board.ts` on the website.

**Variables.** `AZURE_TENANT_ID`, `AZURE_APP_ID` and `AZURE_CLIENT_SECRET` on the api. This route has no fallback to the `AZURE_GRAPH_*` names.

**When it fails.** Unit pages show no board. With a variable missing, the api logs `Missing Azure credentials in environment variables`.

**Test it safely.** The route only reads, so running it from a local api with the real variables changes nothing in Microsoft. It still reads real staff names, emails and photos, so keep what it returns off screenshots and out of issues. It also uses production credentials: get them from the account owner for the session, and don't keep them in `.env.local` afterwards.

## SharePoint

**What it does.** Governing documents. The admin uploads each adopted version to one document library in SharePoint, and the website streams downloads from it.

**Code.** `packages/connectors/src/sharepoint/index.ts`. The admin side is `apps/admin/src/app/(portal)/_actions/documents.ts` and `apps/admin/src/lib/documents/sharepoint-mapping.ts`. The website's download route is `apps/web/src/app/api/documents/[id]/download/route.ts`.

**Variables.** `SHAREPOINT_TENANT_ID`, `SHAREPOINT_CLIENT_ID`, `SHAREPOINT_CLIENT_SECRET` and `SHAREPOINT_SITES` on the website and the admin. `SHAREPOINT_DOCUMENTS_DRIVE_ID`, on the admin only, names the library that uploads go to. It has no fallback on purpose: a comment in `sharepoint-mapping.ts` says guessing the library once sent uploads to the wrong site.

**When it fails.**

- On the website, a document download answers `500` with `{"error":"Download failed"}`, and the log says `Document download error:`.
- In the admin, an upload shows `SharePoint is not configured: …` or `SharePoint upload failed: …`.

**Test it safely.** No test library is recorded. An upload with the real variables writes to BISO's real library. For an end-to-end test, ask a tenant administrator for a separate document library, and put its drive id in `SHAREPOINT_DOCUMENTS_DRIVE_ID` in your own `apps/admin/.env.local`. The unit tests are `packages/connectors/src/sharepoint/index.test.ts` and `apps/admin/src/lib/documents/sharepoint-mapping.test.ts`.

## Teams approval bot

**What it does.** It's part of direct ledger posting for reimbursements, which runs only while the feature flag `expenses_ledger_posting` is on. It's off by default. With it on, a submitted reimbursement goes through an approval chain. Each approver gets a Teams card from the bot and an Outlook email with a link to the website's approval page (`/fs/approve/<token>`). A decision on the card comes back to the api at `POST /api/teams/bot`. With the flag off, nothing uses the bot, and submissions are emailed to accounting instead. [Accounting](/developers/reference/accounting#reimbursements) has the rest of the flow.

**Code.** `packages/connectors/src/teams-bot/`, `apps/api/src/app/api/teams/bot/route.ts` and `apps/api/src/lib/expense-approval.ts`. The card's extra data comes from `apps/api/src/lib/expense-card-details.ts`. The Teams app package is in `apps/api/teams-manifest/`. Its README lists the bot's Graph permissions, and says the package has to be uploaded to the organisation's Teams app catalog by hand. The approvers are found with the [Microsoft Graph](#microsoft-graph) registration, by the rules under "Who approves" below.

**What the card shows.** `loadApprovalCardDetails` (`expense-card-details.ts`) runs for every notification: the first step, the next step after a decision, and a resend. The next approver therefore sees what earlier steps changed. The card and the email list the receipts (type, account, amount; supporting rows with no amount are left out), the AI and system hints, the six latest history lines with the newest first, the submitter's self-declaration, and a note when the national approver decides. The expense, the receipts and the history are read at the same time. A hint about one receipt (it has an `attachmentId`) starts with that receipt's description, or its type when it has none, cut to 40 characters, and a colon. The national note comes from the expense's plan (`getCampusApprovalPlan`): when the plan has a president step, the national approver stands in for the president, and the note reads `The campus president could not be found or is the submitter; the national approver decides instead.` Any other national step on campuses 1 to 4 stands in for a controller who submitted, and the note reads `The national approver decides in place of the usual approver.` The hint and history sentences are English copies written in the api, because it has no i18n. The hint sentences mirror `adminPortal.expenses.hintsText.*`, so change both together. A receipt with an amount and no account turns Approve into a link to the admin page (`EXPENSE_ADMIN_URL` plus `/expenses/<id>`), where the account is set. Every lookup is best-effort: one that fails leaves its part empty and the card still goes out. The receipts read is the exception that fails closed: when it fails, the card has no receipts and offers the admin link instead of Approve. A card too large for Teams is cut down, and last of all replaced by a minimal card.

**The approval page.** The email's link opens `/fs/approve/<token>` on the website (`apps/web/src/app/(public)/fs/approve/[token]/approval-client.tsx`). It reads `GET /api/expenses/approve?token=…`, whose context has `missingAccount` and `adminUrl`. When `missingAccount` is true, the page shows the `common.approval.accountMissing` note with a link to `adminUrl` in place of Approve. Reject stays. The api checks too: `decideApproval` refuses an approve by link while any receipt with an amount has no account. `POST /api/expenses/approve` then answers 400 with `{ "code": "account_missing", "error": "A receipt has no account yet. Set it in admin before approving." }`, and a click on an old Teams card gets the same sentence. A rejection is never blocked.

**Updating a sent card.** The api stores the chat and activity id of each sent card on the step. When a step is decided on the website or in the admin, `decideStep` calls `updateProactiveCard` to replace the card with the decision result, so a stale card can't be clicked. It does this last, after the reimbursement's status is written, the next approver is notified or the reimbursement is finalized, and gives Teams 5 seconds (`CARD_UPDATE_TIMEOUT_MS`). A decision made on the card itself keeps the normal invoke response and does not call it. A failed or timed-out update is logged as `[expense-approval] Teams card update failed:` and never fails the decision.

**Who approves.** `getCampusApprovalPlan` (`packages/shared/utils/expense-approvers.ts`) gives the ordered steps, and `resolveExpenseApprovers` (`expense-approver-resolution.ts`) turns each into a person.

- **Plan.** National (campus 5): the national approver. Oslo (campus 1), ordinary departments: the department's financial manager, then the campus controller. Drift Campus and Ledelsen on campuses 1 to 4 (Finago department ids 1 and 2, 300 and 301, 600 and 601, 800 and 801): the campus controller alone. Other departments on Bergen, Trondheim and Stavanger: the campus controller. An unknown campus id throws.
- **Switches.** `approvalSwitchFor` says which self-declaration a department offers: `controller` on the leadership departments, `financial_manager` on the other Oslo departments, none elsewhere. The submit payload carries `submitter_is_controller` and `submitter_is_financial_manager`, and `expense-payload.ts` keeps a flag only when the department offers that switch. The app sends no controller flag. On a new report that reads as false. On a saved draft, `mergeLegacyDraft` keeps the stored flag only while the payload's campus and department are the draft's, so a draft switched on from the web and then submitted from the app still goes to the president. A payload for another campus or department drops it, because a flag declared for one department must not carry over to another. The draft and submit routes both read `submitter_is_controller` with the stored draft for that. The submit route routes from the row as saved, so the chain, the stored flags and the declaration note on the `submitted` event always agree. The financial-manager switch swaps the first step to the department manager, with the deputy as the fallback. The controller switch swaps the controller step for the campus president, so a controller never approves their own claim. The flags are self-declared and can't be checked, because members submit from personal accounts. The web shows the switch in `expense-report.tsx`. The Flutter app has no controller switch yet.
- **Resolution order, per step.** The directory first, then the AI lookup for department roles and the president, then the default mailbox for the controller and the national approver. `DEFAULT_DIRECTORY_EMAILS` holds those defaults. The steps resolve in parallel, and the numbers follow the plan.
- **Directory.** The table `expense_approvers` (`expense-approver-directory.ts`) is keyed by `directoryKey`: `dept:<departmentId>:<role>` for a department and `campus:<campusId>:<role>` for a campus. A row has the email, the Azure AD id, the display name and a `source`: `seed`, `ai`, `admin` or `confirmed`. A lookup reads only active rows. A save never overwrites a row whose source is `admin` or `confirmed`. A controller or national step with no row saves the default as `seed`. Using a row sets `last_used_at`. Admins edit rows in the admin's **Godkjennere** (Approvers) tab, `apps/admin/src/app/(portal)/expenses/settings/approvers/`. [Fix expense approval issues](/guide/access-and-settings/expense-approvals#the-approver-directory) says who may edit what. A save there with a new email clears `confidence` and `confirmed_at`, and looks the address up in Graph for the new `aad_id`, with a 6-second limit. When the lookup fails or finds nobody, `aad_id` is empty and that approver gets the email only, with no Teams card.
- **Lookup** (`expense-approver-lookup.ts`). It collects candidates from Graph in two ways, run at the same time with `Promise.allSettled`, so one that fails leaves the others' candidates. The first is users whose `department` equals the department name and whose address starts with the role's prefix (`findUsersByDepartment`). This pass has no campus check: it relies on the department name, which carries the campus prefix, as in `OSL Næringslivsutvalget`. The second is a search for each prefix (`searchUsers`), kept when the address has a campus slug from `CAMPUS_MAILBOX_SLUGS` and, for department roles, a segment that starts with a hint taken from the department name (`abbreviation` is empty in the data, so the hints come from the name). An account already found by its department attribute skips the hint check (`matchesMailbox` gets `hints = null`), but still needs the campus slug when it turns up in the address search. For the president the department attribute is checked too, and the address search uses the campus slug alone. Up to 25 candidates go to the fast model, which picks one email and a confidence. Only 0.8 or more counts, and the answer is saved as `ai`. The candidate list is sent as data, not as instructions.
- **Time limit** (`expense-approver-resolution.ts`). Resolution runs inside submit, and the app gives up after 20 seconds. Each AI lookup, the Graph queries and the model call together, has one 8-second deadline: an `AbortSignal.timeout` that the model call gets and that a `Promise.race` holds against the whole lookup. A failure, a Graph call that never answers or the deadline counts as not found.
- **Missed lookups.** A directory key the AI lookup found nobody for, because no answer reached 0.8 or the deadline passed, isn't looked up again for an hour. A lookup that failed, because Graph or the model returned an error, isn't remembered, so the next submission tries again. The step falls back as it would have, with its issue. The misses live in a `Map` in the api process, so a restart or another instance looks again. A find for that key, in the directory or by the lookup, clears its miss. So an approver account fixed in Microsoft 365 can take up to an hour to be found, while a row added to the directory is used at once.
- **Deactivated entries.** A directory row with `active` false is never switched on or overwritten by the api, and the AI lookup skips its key. A department step whose entry is deactivated is dropped with the issue `The <role> entry is deactivated in the directory`, and the controller step still approves. A deactivated controller or national entry still routes to the default mailbox, because a campus always needs one, with the issue `…; sent to the default <email>`. A deactivated president entry goes to the national approver, with `The president entry is deactivated in the directory; sent to the national approver (<email>)`.
- **Manager and deputy.** The order is the directory's manager, then a confident AI manager, then the directory's deputy, then a confident AI deputy. A manager in the directory ends the search. Otherwise the AI manager and the deputy are looked up at once, and the first found in that order is used.
- **When nobody is found.** A department step with no approver is dropped, an issue is recorded and the controller step still approves. A president nobody can fill goes to the national approver, with an issue. That step has the source `default` and `fallbackFor: "president"`, wherever the national mailbox came from. The controller is never used as the fallback. A missing department id records `Expense has no department set.` and drops the step. If no step resolves at all, `createApprovalChain` records `No approver could be resolved` before it sets the reimbursement to pending.
- **Self and duplicate guard** (`guardChain` in `expense-approval-guard.ts`). After resolution, `createApprovalChain` compares each step with the email on the submitter's `user` row, which it reads while the approvers resolve. A step whose approver is the submitter takes the first stand-in that isn't the submitter: for the controller, the campus default mailbox, then the national mailbox; for the president, the national mailbox, marked `fallbackFor: "president"`; for the national approver, the national mailbox (`DEFAULT_DIRECTORY_EMAILS["5"].national`). A stand-in row has no `directory_key`, so approving never confirms the submitter's own directory row. A department step has no stand-in and is dropped, so the controller step approves. Each records `Approver is the submitter`, or `Approver is the submitter; sent to <email> instead`. Of two steps with the same approver, the earlier is dropped with `Same approver as the <role> step; the earlier <role> step was dropped`. The steps left are numbered from 1. When nothing is left, the reimbursement goes to pending with `No approver could be resolved`, as when no step resolves.
- **Issues and history.** `createApprovalChain` writes the issues after the chain rows. Those rows are the claim, through the unique (`expense_id`, `step`) index, so a duplicate submit that loses the race writes none. Each resolved step also writes an `approver_resolved` event, with the role and source (`directory`, `ai` or `default`) in the note, as in `finance via ai`. The president fallback reads `national (president fallback)`.
- **Confirming.** A decision to approve calls `confirmDirectoryApprover` for the step's `directory_key`. It marks the row `confirmed` only when the step's email equals the row's email. A rejection doesn't.

**Variables.** `TEAMS_BOT_APP_ID`, `TEAMS_BOT_APP_PASSWORD`, `TEAMS_BOT_APP_TENANT_ID`, `TEAMS_APP_ID`, `TEAMS_BOT_MAIL_FROM` and `TEAMS_BOT_SERVICE_URL`, all on the api. `EXPENSE_APPROVAL_WEB_URL` sets the website address in the approval links, and `EXPENSE_ADMIN_URL` the admin address in the card's admin link.

**When it fails.**

- The card and the email are sent separately. If one fails, the log says `[expense-approval] Teams notify failed:` or `[expense-approval] Email notify failed:`, and the other is still sent.
- If neither reaches the first approver, the submission fails, and the reimbursement goes back to draft so the student can try again.
- If an approver can't be found, or a later approver can't be reached, the api records an issue. Staff see it in the admin under **Utlegg** (Expenses), in the **Trenger oppfølging** (Needs attention) view (`/expenses?view=attention`), and a global admin or the campus controller can send the approval again from there. The old address `/it/expense-approvals` redirects there.
- A card click that fails the bot's sign-in check answers `401`, and the api logs `[teams/bot] auth failed:`.

**Test it safely.** There's no safe end-to-end test. The bot's messaging endpoint is set to the production API in its Azure resource (`apps/api/teams-manifest/README.md`), so Teams sends every card click there. Keep `expenses_ledger_posting` off in your own project, and nothing calls the bot. The approver rules have unit tests in `packages/shared/utils/expense-approvers.test.ts`.

## Azure Automation

**What it does.** The admin's board turnover for role accounts calls a runbook in Azure Automation through two webhooks, to start a retention run and to stop it 7 days later. [The August board turnover](/developers/keep-it-running/yearly-calendar#the-august-board-turnover) describes the whole flow and what the repo doesn't hold, and [Azure Automation webhooks](/developers/keep-it-running/yearly-calendar#azure-automation-webhooks) covers their end dates.

**Code.** `apps/admin/src/app/(portal)/_actions/it-turnover.ts`, `apps/admin/src/lib/it/turnover.ts` and the stop job `apps/admin/src/app/api/it/turnover/stop-retention/route.ts`. The runbook itself isn't in the repo.

**Variables.** `AZURE_RETENTION_START_WEBHOOK_URL` and `AZURE_RETENTION_STOP_WEBHOOK_URL` on the admin. Each URL carries its own token, so treat it as a secret. `AZURE_ACCOUNT_TURNOVER_WEBHOOK_URL` on the api belongs to an older route that nothing calls.

**When it fails.**

- If the start fails, the turnover still renames the account. The job in `m365_turnover_jobs` is left as `retention_start_failed`, and the dialog lists it under **Krever manuell oppfølging:** (Needs manual follow-up).
- If the stop fails 7 days later, the job shows `stop_failed`, and every scheduled-dispatch run tries the stop again.

**Test it safely.** There's no safe end-to-end test. A turnover renames a real Microsoft account, resets its MFA and sets a new password, so never run one to test. The state rules have unit tests in `apps/admin/src/lib/it/turnover.test.ts`.

## BI student sign-in

**What it does.** Students sign in to the website and the app with their BI account, through Appwrite's `oidc` provider. That provider reads its settings from two api routes:

- `/api/auth/bi-oidc/openid-configuration` serves the discovery document of BI's tenant, from `BI_OIDC_WELL_KNOWN_URL`, with the user-info address swapped for the api's own.
- `/api/auth/bi-userinfo` passes the request on to Microsoft's user-info endpoint, and marks a `bi.no` email as verified in the answer. Microsoft never sends that claim, and without it Appwrite refuses to attach a BI sign-in to an existing account with the same email.

[Auth and roles](/developers/change-it/auth-and-roles#how-students-sign-in) explains why.

**Code.** `apps/api/src/app/api/auth/` and `apps/api/src/lib/bi-oidc-discovery.ts`, `apps/api/src/lib/bi-userinfo.ts`. On the website, `signInWithBiStudent()` in `apps/web/src/lib/server.ts` and the return route `apps/web/src/app/(auth)/auth/oauth/route.ts`.

**Credentials.** The provider's client id and secret are set on the `oidc` provider in the Appwrite console, next to the discovery address. They aren't variables.

**When it fails.**

- If the api is down, BI sign-in fails on the website and in the app.
- The website's sign-in page shows **Vi kunne ikke logge deg inn med BI. Prøv igjen, eller bruk en innloggingslenke.** (We could not sign you in with BI. Please try again, or use a login link.)
- Without `BI_OIDC_WELL_KNOWN_URL`, the discovery route answers `500` with `not_configured` and logs `[bi-oidc] BI_OIDC_WELL_KNOWN_URL is not configured`. If Microsoft can't be reached, it answers `502`.

**Test it safely.** There's no safe end-to-end test. A comment in `packages/shared/utils/bi-identity-sync.ts` says account linking can't be tried from `localhost` and only works against a real deployment. The routes have unit tests next to them in `apps/api/src/app/api/auth/`, and the website's side is tested in `apps/web/src/lib/bi-login.test.ts`. Don't use `BI_DEV_STUDENT_EMAIL_OVERRIDE` to test: production is the only deployment, and the override lets one address claim a student's identity there ([Secrets and environment](/developers/keep-it-running/secrets-and-environment#bi-sign-in)).

## BI directory

**What it does.** After a BI sign-in, the website, or the api for the app (`/api/auth/bi-sync`), looks the student up in BI's directory through Graph. It needs the student's `employeeId`, which 24SevenOffice uses to find the student's customer record. The office location and department are read only as a hint for the campus.

**Code.** `packages/connectors/src/azure/bi-directory.ts` and `packages/shared/utils/bi-identity-sync.ts`.

**Variables.** `BI_AZURE_TENANT_ID`, `BI_AZURE_CLIENT_ID` and `BI_AZURE_CLIENT_SECRET`, on the website and the api. They address BISO's app registration in BI's tenant, not BISO's own tenant.

**When it fails.** The student is still signed in. Their `bi_employee_id` stays empty, and the membership page says **Vi kunne ikke bekrefte BI-registreringen din** (We couldn't verify your BI record), without charging anything. Membership purchase is blocked until the lookup works, either at a later sign-in or when the student presses **Prøv igjen** (Try again) on that page.

**Test it safely.** Same as [BI student sign-in](#bi-student-sign-in): only on a real deployment. The lookup has unit tests in `apps/web/src/lib/actions/bi-identity.test.ts`.
