# Creating vacancies

A vacancy is the public-facing job posting *plus* the back-office settings that decide how applications are collected, screened, and scheduled. You set everything up once on the job editor, and the rest of the recruitment round runs from there.

## Opening the editor

From the Jobs list, open an existing vacancy to edit it, or create a new one. The editor lives at `/jobs/[id]` and is organised into sections you can fill in at your own pace. A vacancy starts as a **draft**, which means it is invisible on biso.no until you publish it.

## What you fill in

A vacancy has more configuration than a typical news article because it has to drive the whole application flow. The sections below are the ones that matter most.

### Basic details

The headline metadata that defines the role.

| Field | What it does |
|-------|--------------|
| Title | The role name shown on biso.no. |
| Campus | Which campus the role belongs to. Locked to your campus if you are not a global admin. |
| Department | Which BISO department owns this role. |
| Application deadline | The cut-off date and time. After this, the public form is closed. |
| Status | Draft, published, or closed. |

### Description

A rich-text description of the role: responsibilities, requirements, the team, why someone should apply. This is what candidates read before deciding to apply. Keep it honest and specific — vague descriptions get vague applications.

### Custom application questions

You can add **custom questions** that applicants must answer in addition to uploading a resume. These are the prompts you actually care about ("Why this role?", "Describe a time you led a project", "Which campus events have you been to?").

Each question is stored on the vacancy and the answers come back attached to every application, so the whole pipeline knows what to display. Keep questions focused — three to six good prompts get better answers than a long list of generic ones.

### Screening rubric

The **screening rubric** is how you tell the AI what a good candidate looks like for *this specific* role. It is a list of criteria with weights — for example, "experience organising student events", "comfortable with public speaking", "available evenings during the semester".

The rubric is only used if you turn on auto-screening (see below). You can still define it and leave auto-screening off — it then acts as a written reminder of what you are evaluating against.

For the full story of how the rubric translates into a score, see [AI screening](/docs/admin-handbook/jobs/ai-screening).

### Auto-screen toggle

A simple on/off switch. When **on**, every incoming application is automatically screened against the rubric and given a score before you ever open it. When **off**, applications arrive untouched and you review them yourself.

You can leave this off during the first few applications, look at them manually, and then turn it on once you have a feel for the role.

### Interview template

The interview template controls how interviews for this vacancy work:

- The default **duration** of an interview slot.
- The **questions** you plan to ask, so every interviewer covers the same ground.

The template is what powers the candidate's booking experience later — see [Interviews and booking](/docs/admin-handbook/jobs/interviews-and-booking) for the full flow.

## Publishing

Once the vacancy is written, switch it from **draft** to **published**. From that moment:

- The posting appears on biso.no under the campus's open positions.
- The application form goes live, with your custom questions.
- Resumes uploaded by applicants are stored securely and only accessible to admins who can manage this vacancy.

If you spot a typo after publishing, just edit the vacancy in place — there is no separate draft cycle for jobs.

## Closing a vacancy

When the application deadline passes (or you have found the person you want), move the vacancy to **closed**. Closing:

- Removes the role from biso.no.
- Stops new applications from being accepted.
- Leaves all existing applications fully manageable — you can still move them through to offer and hired.

Closed vacancies stay searchable in the Jobs list, so the round is never lost.

## Common pitfalls

A few things worth checking before you publish:

- **Campus and department** are set correctly. Once applications start arriving, they inherit the scoping, which decides who can see them.
- **Application deadline** is in the right time zone. The deadline is stored exactly as you set it.
- **Custom questions** are answerable in a paragraph or two. Multi-part questions tend to get partial answers.
- If you turn on **auto-screen**, the rubric is filled in. An empty rubric means the AI has nothing to score against.
