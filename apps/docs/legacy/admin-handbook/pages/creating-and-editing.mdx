# Creating and editing pages

This page walks you through the day-to-day rhythm of working in the editor: starting a new page, opening an old one, adding and arranging blocks, and saving your changes.

## Starting a new page

1. From the sidebar, open **Pages**.
2. Click **New page** in the top right. The editor opens on a blank canvas.
3. With nothing selected in the canvas, the inspector on the right shows the page metadata. Fill in:
   - **Title** — the heading that appears in the browser tab and on the page itself.
   - **Slug** — the URL path on biso.no (e.g. `about-us` becomes `biso.no/about-us`). Keep it short and lowercase, no spaces.
   - **Description** — one or two sentences. This is what Google and link previews show.
   - **Department** — pick the department that owns this page. If you're a department lead, your own department is pre-selected.
   - **Theme** — optional. Two colours that restyle the page. See [Themes](#themes) below.
4. Hit **Save**. The page now exists as a draft. It's not visible to the public yet — see [Drafts and publishing](/docs/admin-handbook/pages/drafts-and-publishing).

## Opening an existing page

From the **Pages** list, click the page you want to edit. The list shows the page's title, slug, owning department, status (draft / published), and when it was last edited.

If you can't find a page, check:

- **Your campus scope** — a campus admin only sees their own campus's pages. A global admin can switch campus from the topbar to see another campus's pages.
- **The search bar** — the list is filtered as you type.
- **Drafts** — recently created pages that haven't been published yet also show up in [Drafts](/docs/admin-handbook/drafts).

## Sections and columns

A page is a **header** at the top, followed by **sections**. Each section is a band across the page with one to four **columns**, and the blocks go inside the columns. To put things side by side, add a section with two, three or four columns. On a tablet some layouts can stack instead, and on a phone the columns always stack.

- **Add a section** from the layout picker on the canvas, then drop blocks into its columns.
- **Move a section** by dragging it. You can also drag a block between columns, or onto the gap between two sections to give it a section of its own.
- **Section settings** appear in the inspector when you select a section: columns, background (white, tinted, dark or photo), spacing, how columns line up, whether it stays side by side on a tablet, which column comes first on a phone, hide on phone, and a link name for jumping to it. You can also duplicate or delete the section.
- The **Outline** tab in the inspector shows the whole page as a tree.

## Adding a block

Open the **Palette** on the left. Blocks are grouped into categories — "Top of page", "Tell a story", "Show people & numbers", and so on. You can browse a category or type into the search box.

To add a block, drag it from the palette onto the canvas. Drop it where you want it: in a column, between two existing blocks, or in a new section. A blue insertion line shows where it will land.

You can also click a block in the palette to drop it at the bottom of the chosen column.

For a full list of what each block does, see the [Blocks reference](/docs/admin-handbook/pages/blocks-reference).

## Editing a block

Click a block on the canvas to select it. A coloured outline appears around it, and the inspector on the right swaps from page metadata to that block's properties.

What you'll find in the inspector depends on the block:

- **Text blocks** — a rich-text editor (bold, italic, links, headings, lists).
- **List blocks** (events, jobs, news, partners, etc.) — a "source" picker that decides which items to pull. You can filter by campus, by department, or pick a preset like "upcoming events".
- **Media blocks** (image, gallery, video) — an upload area. Files are stored in the BISO media library.
- **Layout blocks** (tabs, two-column, feature grid) — slots inside the block that hold further content.

To get back to page-level fields, click an empty area of the canvas to deselect.

## Rearranging and removing blocks

- **Move**: hover over a block and grab its handle (the dots on the left edge). Drag it up or down.
- **Delete**: select the block and press **Delete** or **Backspace**, or use the delete button in the inspector.
- **Undo / redo**: `Ctrl + Z` (or `Cmd + Z` on Mac) and `Ctrl + Shift + Z` to redo.

<Callout type="warning">
Undo history lives in memory only. If you refresh the page, you lose the ability to undo — but your draft itself is safe, because it's auto-saved.
</Callout>

## Themes

By default a page uses BISO's navy and its normal accent colour. A page can carry its own **theme**: a **dark** colour and an **accent** colour. The dark colour paints the menu bar, the page header, dark and photo sections and the footer. The accent colour is used for buttons, links and big numbers.

To change it, deselect everything on the canvas and open the **Theme** panel in the page settings:

1. Pick a colour for **Dark** and one for **Accent**. Drag in the colour square and the hue slider, or type a hex code such as `#3b1c6b`. If the code isn't a colour, the field says "Not a colour". The BISO colours are there as quick picks.
2. Watch the result line under each colour. It shows the contrast and a tick when the colour is fine.
3. Use **Reset** to go back to BISO's colours.

The panel tells you who is affected. For a page that belongs to a department it says "Applies to all *department* pages": when you publish, the theme is copied to the department, so all of its pages follow it. A page with no department says "Applies to this page". A page with no theme of its own follows its department's theme.

### Contrast rules

Text has to stay readable, so the editor checks both colours:

- **Dark** must be dark enough for white text to read on it (contrast of at least 7 to 1). If not, it says "Too light for white text".
- **Accent** must stand out on the dark colour (at least 4.5 to 1). If not, it says "Doesn't stand out on the dark colour".

When a colour fails, a button appears: **Use #xxxxxx**. It offers the **nearest shade that passes**, the same hue with only the lightness changed. Click it to accept, or pick something else.

<Callout type="warning">
While a colour fails the check, the page can't be saved or published. The topbar says "Not saved: theme colours fail the contrast check", and Publish and the language switch are disabled until you fix the colour or press **Reset**.
</Callout>

Body text on white stays navy whatever theme you choose. If a page ends up looking wrong after you publish a theme, press **Reset** and publish again. Unpublishing a page does not remove the theme from its department.

## Project pages

BISO's big projects (Fadderullan, Karrieredagene, Bergensbaneløpet and Winter Games) have their own addresses: `biso.no/projects/<project>`. Until now these were fixed pages built by IT. A project can now have a page made in the editor, saved under the slug `projects/<project>` (for example `projects/winter-games`). When a published page exists at that exact address, the site shows it. Until then the site keeps showing the ready-made project page.

Who edits it depends on the project:

- **Winter Games** is a national project. Its page belongs to the Wintergames unit, and the Wintergames board can edit and publish it, even though board members are spread across campuses. The slug and department are locked, because they tie the page to the unit.
- **Fadderullan, Karrieredagene and Bergensbaneløpet** have a unit on each campus, so their pages belong to no single department. Only a global admin can create, edit, publish, unpublish or archive them.

Everything under `projects/` is reserved for these pages. You can't use that address for an ordinary page. If you hit that message, open the unit under Departments and use **Create page**, or ask BISO IT for a project hub page. Once a national project's page is published, the unit's own address sends visitors to `/projects/<project>`.

## Uploading large files

Images, videos and documents are uploaded from the inspector of the block that uses them. The limits are:

| File | Types | Largest |
|------|-------|---------|
| Image | JPG, PNG, GIF, WebP, SVG | 10 MB |
| Video | MP4, MOV, WebM | 250 MB |
| Document | PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, CSV, TXT | 25 MB |

Files bigger than 5 MB are sent in pieces automatically. You don't do anything differently, but a big video takes a while, and there is no progress bar yet: wait until the file appears in the block before you close the tab or leave the page. If an upload fails, try again. If it keeps failing, tell BISO IT.

Videos should be web-friendly: H.264 MP4 at 1080p or smaller loads fastest. A looping video in a page header is always muted, and visitors who have asked their device to reduce motion see the still poster image instead, so always add a poster image.

## Previewing

In the topbar, switch from **Edit** to **Preview** to see the page as a visitor would, without the editor chrome. Use the **viewport toggle** (desktop / tablet / mobile) to check how it looks at different screen sizes.

## Saving

Saving is **automatic**. A few hundred milliseconds after you stop typing or dragging, the draft writes itself to the server. The save indicator in the topbar tells you the current state:

- **Saved** — everything is on the server.
- **Saving…** — a save is in flight.
- **Error** — something went wrong. Try the **Save** button to retry; if it keeps failing, copy your text somewhere safe and tell IT.

The **Save** button is still useful for forcing an immediate save (e.g. before closing the tab on a slow connection).

When you're ready to publish, head to [Drafts and publishing](/docs/admin-handbook/pages/drafts-and-publishing).
